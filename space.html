03 · Space technology

Looking up, with better tools.

Satellites, rovers, telescopes. A quick tour of the tech that lets us study things millions of kilometres away.

Start exploring↓
somewhere out there

Why bother?

Why go to space?

Space research teaches us about planets, stars and how our own solar system formed. It pays off at home too. Satellites handle GPS, phone signals and weather forecasts, and they help track things like forests and melting ice. Studying other worlds also hints at what conditions life might need.

The toolkit

What gets
us there.

Getting up

Rockets and probes

Rockets get things off the ground. Probes and landers do the actual visiting once they arrive.

In orbit

Satellites

Thousands circle Earth right now, handling maps, TV, weather data and internet for remote places.

Looking far

Space telescopes

Up there, there's no blurry atmosphere in the way, so they can see much sharper and much further.

Big moments

A few
milestones.

1969

Apollo 11

Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin became the first people to walk on the Moon.

1990

Hubble launches

It went up with a flawed mirror, astronauts fixed it in 1993, and it's been sending back landmark images ever since.

2021

James Webb Space Telescope

Launched on Christmas Day. It looks at the universe in infrared, which lets it see very old, very distant galaxies.

2023

Chandrayaan-3

India landed near the Moon's south pole in August and rolled out the Pragyan rover.

What's next

Lots left to find.

Mars, icy moons, planets around other stars. Nobody knows yet what's out there, which is kind of the whole point.
