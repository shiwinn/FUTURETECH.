The basics

So what is AI?

AI is a loose label for programs that do things we'd normally expect a person to do: spot a face in a photo, translate a sentence, guess which song you'll like next. It isn't one technology and it definitely isn't a digital brain. Most systems are great at one narrow job and hopeless at everything else.

You've already used it

AI in
real life.

Health

Second pair of eyes

Doctors use AI tools to flag odd spots on scans. A person still makes the call. The software just helps them look.

School

Study helper

Apps can turn a chapter into practice questions, or explain something a second way when the first one didn't click. It does get things wrong, so check it.

Daily life

Quiet stuff

Spam filters, autocorrect, maps rerouting you around traffic. You've been using AI for years without calling it that.

How it works

Examples in,
guess out.

1

Study examples

The model is shown loads of examples, like photos labelled "cat" or "not a cat", until it picks up what they have in common.

2

Meet something new

You give it a photo, a question or a sound it has never seen before.

3

Make a guess

It gives its best guess. Best guess, not guaranteed truth, which is why a human should check anything important.

The catch

It's a good guesser. That's the problem.

AI can be confidently wrong, pick up bias from the data it learned from, and often wants a lot of your personal info. So the useful questions are: who's checking it, who's it unfair to, and where does your data go?
