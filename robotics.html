02 · Robotics

Machines that do real things.

A robot is basically a computer with a body. It senses what's around it, decides what to do, then moves. Here's how that works and where you'll find them.

Meet the machines↓
sense · decide · move

The basics

What is robotics?

Robotics sits where mechanical engineering, electronics and programming meet. Some robots repeat one move perfectly all day. Others use cameras and sensors to react to whatever's around them. The useful ones mostly work next to people, not instead of them.

Where they work

Robots on
the job.

Factories

Tireless arms

They weld, paint and assemble car parts, and the thousandth one comes out as accurate as the first.

Hospitals

Steady hands

Surgeons use robotic systems for tiny, very steady movements, always with a person in control. Others help people through physical rehab.

Out there

Go-anywhere explorers

Rovers on Mars, drones over wildfires, small subs checking pipelines. Basically anywhere too far, hot or risky for a person.

Under the hood

Three parts,
one robot.

1

Sensors

Cameras, distance sensors and touch sensors tell the robot what's around it.

2

Controller

Software takes that info and picks the next move, inside limits its builders set.

3

Actuators

Motors turn the decision into movement, and feedback tells the robot whether it actually got there.

What's next

Robots already work where we can't.

Disaster zones, deep water, other planets. They do best when they're built with safety in mind and a person who can step in. The farthest ones are a long way from any factory.
